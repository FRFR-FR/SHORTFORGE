# Window Tax Short — Production Checklists

## 1. Research and script checklist

- [x] Original subject, title and narration; no dialogue or script copied from another creator.
- [x] Hook presents a clear, unexpected premise in the first sentence.
- [x] Establish the year and location before explaining the mechanism.
- [x] Explain the count-based, banded assessment without implying a single universal price.
- [x] Show a concrete response: some owners closed windows.
- [x] Include the archival nuance: a blank window can also be a symmetry/design choice.
- [x] Resolve the story with the 1851 repeal and concerns about light and ventilation.
- [x] Source every historical claim in the production notes.
- [ ] Verify spoken narration and captions match after TTS generation.

## 2. Visual and screen checklist

**Opening / first 5 seconds**
- [ ] Readable vertical establishing image of a Georgian street.
- [ ] Title does not obscure the focal window or the face.
- [ ] Immediate visual question: windows are being counted.

**Middle / mechanism**
- [ ] The assessor's tally and ledger read clearly on a phone-sized screen.
- [ ] Show the bill as a consequence of the window count.
- [ ] Brick animation is legible: a specific opening is filled, not the whole building.
- [ ] The closed opening stays visually distinct from ordinary windows.
- [ ] The symmetry example shows a deliberate blank window within a balanced facade.

**Ending / payoff**
- [ ] 1851 repeal is clear in narration/captions.
- [ ] Brightness returns to the facade without implying every blocked window was caused by the tax.
- [ ] Final image is composed to make the idea memorable and the cut clean.

**Whole-screen quality**
- [ ] Main focal point remains inside 9:16 safe margins.
- [ ] Foreground, subject and facade have clear depth separation.
- [ ] No important action is hidden behind the host model, captions or props.
- [ ] Text is limited to the opening title and burned-in captions.
- [ ] Palette, materials, exposure and lighting remain consistent across shots.
- [ ] Camera moves are motivated, smooth and readable; no arbitrary shake.
- [ ] Every major object keeps scale and position continuity between cuts.

## 3. Animation and camera checklist

- [ ] Brick pieces use clear anticipation and settle into the opening without popping.
- [ ] Coins/ledger insert has subtle secondary movement only.
- [ ] Character stance has planted feet, weight shift and restrained hand gestures.
- [ ] Camera establishes, pushes in, reveals the mechanism, pauses for the archival nuance and widens for the payoff.
- [ ] Motion stays smooth at 30 fps; no frozen holds or camera jitter.

## 4. Audio checklist

- [ ] Narration is intelligible and mixed consistently.
- [ ] Captions are checked against the finished voice timing.
- [ ] No background music unless specifically requested.
- [ ] No unlicensed sound effects or music.

## 5. Technical and delivery checklist

- [ ] GitHub Actions render completes.
- [ ] Exactly 1,800 frames are generated and analyzed.
- [ ] Frame QA reports no blank frames or long frozen runs.
- [ ] H.264 MP4 is verified by ffprobe at 720×1280, 30 fps and 60 seconds.
- [ ] Contact sheet and render manifest are inspected.
- [ ] Seven SHORTFORGE review passes have statuses and frame/time references.
- [ ] Final MP4, QA JSON, contact sheet and manifest are published together.

## 6. Originality benchmark

The external channel research informed only broad format choices: short narrated curiosity-led subject, quick establishment, visual explanation, a factual twist and a clean payoff. This production does not use that creator's video files, thumbnails, scripts, characters, shot order, branding or signature assets. The animation, scene, dialogue and visual plan are original.
